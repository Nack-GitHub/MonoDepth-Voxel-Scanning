\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{R3 beats the pretrained model in every test room.} Chamfer \SI{\rsMETRICCM}{cm}$\rightarrow$
\SI{\rsFTLIDARALLCM}{cm} ($\rsMAINGAIN\times$; \SI{\rsMAINDIFFCM \pm \rsMAINDIFFSD}{cm} better per room,
\rsMAINWINS/\rsMAINN{} rooms, exact Wilcoxon $p=\rsMAINP$, the smallest attainable at $n=6$) and \fscore{}
\rsMETRICF$\rightarrow$\rsFTLIDARALLF{} (Tables~\ref{tab:main}, \ref{tab:mainscene}). Accuracy falls more than
completeness: the pretrained model builds the room too far away (15--31\,\% over-estimation, \S\ref{sec:metric}); R3
brings surfaces back near where they belong.
\item \textbf{The same holds on rooms that took part in nothing.} On the \rsVN{} Validation-fold rooms AbsRel drops
\rsVMETRICABSREL$\rightarrow$\rsVFTALLABSREL{} ($\rsVGAIN\times$; better in \rsVWINS/\rsVN{} rooms, exact Wilcoxon
$p=\rsVP$) and $\delta_1$ rises \rsVMETRICDELTAONE$\rightarrow$\rsVFTALLDELTAONE{} (Table~\ref{tab:exp7},
Fig.~\ref{fig:finetuneabsrel}). The one exception, \rsVEXCEPT{} (AbsRel \rsVEXCEPTPRE$\rightarrow$\rsVEXCEPTFT), is one
of the pretrained model's best rooms; in the other 19, R3's AbsRel is 21--64\,\% of the pretrained one.
\item \textbf{It does not reach a sensor.} R3 is $\sim$\SI{13}{cm} behind iPad LiDAR in Chamfer and
\SI{\rsFTALLGAPSPARSE}{cm} behind a per-frame fit on 200 sparse points; fine-tuning fixes the scale of the
\emph{domain}, not of \emph{the frame being looked at} (\S\ref{sec:whyft}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Teacher ablation and a no-fine-tuning baseline}\label{sec:finetune}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{exp6_finetune_topdown_grid}
\caption{Exp.~6 top-down error maps (rows $=$ scenes; columns $=$ Depth Pro, the three fine-tunes and the pretrained metric model, as labelled above each panel; colour $=$ distance to the
reference, 0--\SI{10}{cm}). The pretrained column is red almost everywhere---the room is built at the wrong distance;
after fine-tuning the error concentrates at object edges and far walls.}
\label{fig:exp6}
\end{figure}
\textbf{The laser teacher is not measurably better than the LiDAR teacher.} R1 \SI{\rsFTFAROCM}{cm}, R3
\SI{\rsFTLIDARALLCM}{cm} and R2 \SI{\rsFTLIDARCM}{cm} all lie within the across-room std (4.0--\SI{4.7}{cm}), and no
row wins every room (R1 beats R3 in three rooms, R3 wins two, one tie; Table~\ref{tab:mainscene}, Fig.~\ref{fig:exp6}).
Because R1 and R2 also differ in the number of views, the honest claim is that a phone's depth sensor can replace the
laser as the teacher at no measurable cost on these rooms---not that the two are equal. Fourteen extra LiDAR-only rooms
(R3 vs.\ R2) do not help measurably either; we keep R3 as the main model because it was best on validation and is the
only recipe that scales without a laser.

\textbf{Knowing the camera does not rescue metric depth.} Depth Pro~\cite{depthpro}, given the true focal length and
no fine-tuning, reaches \SI{\rsDEPTHPROCM}{cm} (\fscore{} \rsDEPTHPROF), worse than the pretrained DA-v2 Metric row: it
predicts a \SI{48}{\degree} field of view where the camera has \SI{62}{\degree}, and even after removing all scale per
frame its AbsRel is 0.24--0.54. We verified the wrapper on a synthetic room with exact ground truth; the claim is about
this model on this low-resolution indoor input, not about intrinsics-conditioned models in general.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The pipeline loses $\sim$\SI{2}{cm}; LiDAR adds $\sim$\SI{1}{cm}} (\SI{\rsLIDARGAPGTCM \pm
\rsLIDARGAPGTSD}{cm}, same in every room). The two differ clearly only at $\tau=$\SI{2}{cm}: iPad LiDAR is wrong at the
2--\SI{4}{cm} level.
\item \textbf{Monocular depth with the right scale per frame (oracle) is \SI{\rsORACLEGAPLIDARCM \pm
\rsORACLEGAPLIDARSD}{cm} behind LiDAR}, and \textbf{200 sparse metric points per frame recover it} (\SI{\rsSPARSEGAP}{cm}
from the oracle, $\rsSPARSEVSSCENE\times$ better than one-off calibration).
\item \textbf{One calibration per scene is $4.5\times$ worse than the oracle, uniformly} (\rsSCENEc--\SI{\rsSCENEb}{cm}
in every room): a property of the model family, not of a failing room (\S\ref{sec:drift}).
\item \textbf{The pretrained metric model is worst} (\SI{\rsMETRICCM}{cm}): the surfaces are all there, in the wrong
place. Kitchen 45261556 is among its best shapes under the oracle (\rsORACLEf) and its worst metric room (\rsMETRICf):
right shape, wrong scale. This is the baseline R3 improves on.
\end{enumerate}

Table~\ref{tab:secondary} summarises the rest. A $13\times$ smaller model (DA-v2 Small) costs only \SI{1.0}{cm} for a
$6\times$ speed-up, whereas one scale per scene costs \SI{17.6}{cm}: model size is not the bottleneck, scale is.
Frame count governs coverage, not accuracy, and LiDAR confidence maps give no measurable gain.
