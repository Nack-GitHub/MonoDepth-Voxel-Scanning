\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% Thai version: 01_introduction_th.tex (main_short_th.tex); keep the two in step.

Scanning a room into a 3D model is already a consumer feature on iPhone~Pro and iPad~Pro (e.g., Apple
RoomPlan~\cite{roomplan}), and it relies on the \emph{LiDAR sensor} for depth in metres. Devices with LiDAR are,
however, a minority: non-Pro iPhones and almost every Android phone have none. Their alternative is \emph{monocular
depth estimation}---depth predicted from a single colour image---which has advanced rapidly from MiDaS~\cite{midas} to
Depth Anything~\cite{da1,da2}. A product team must therefore know \textbf{by how many centimetres the room is wrong if
LiDAR is replaced by a monocular model, and which use cases that still serves}. Standard per-image
benchmarks~\cite{eigen14}, which average the depth error over the pixels of single images, do not answer this, and
end-to-end reconstruction systems~\cite{atlas,neuralrecon,simplerecon} cannot, because they design the whole pipeline
around their own model.

Some recent models output metres directly~\cite{da2,zoedepth,depthpro}, but they are trained on images that do not
come from a phone camera---DA-v2 Metric-Indoor on rendered rooms of the Hypersim dataset~\cite{hypersim}---and in our
experiments a room reconstructed with DA-v2 Metric-Indoor is \SI{\rsMETRICCM}{cm} wrong on average. The direct remedy
is to \emph{fine-tune} the model on depth from the target device, which raises the question of where these labels come
from: a survey-grade laser scanner~\cite{faro}, which few app makers own, or the \emph{LiDAR already inside Pro
devices}, whose depth an app can collect during normal use. If the latter works, devices with LiDAR can teach those
without it.

We therefore ask whether DA-v2 Metric-Indoor fine-tuned with iPad LiDAR depth, with no laser scanner at training time,
is more accurate than the same model pretrained, when both are measured against the Faro laser scanner that provides
the ground truth of ARKitScenes~\cite{arkitscenes} on rooms never used for training. The study has four objectives:
(1)~to fine-tune the model on iPad LiDAR depth from 24 rooms, giving \ftmain{}, and compare it with the pretrained
model on held-out rooms, counting it as better if its mean error is lower and the per-room difference is significant
at the 5\,\% level under an exact Wilcoxon signed-rank test~\cite{wilcoxon}; (2)~to measure whether the source of the
labels matters, by comparing Faro with LiDAR labels on the same 10 rooms (\ftfaro{} and \ftlidar{}), 10 with 24 rooms
of LiDAR labels, and a model that uses the camera's focal length instead of in-domain labels (Depth
Pro~\cite{depthpro}); (3)~to place \ftmain{} among the depth sources available at run time, namely iPad LiDAR and a
relative model scaled per frame on sparse points or once per room; and (4)~to find the error that fine-tuning leaves,
by fitting a depth scale to every frame and testing whether it follows time, which would indicate drift, or what the
view shows.

Every comparison is scored in two ways. As a room mesh, it is scored by the Chamfer distance~\cite{chamfer}, the mean
distance from each surface to the nearest point of the other, and by the F-score at \SI{5}{cm} (\fscore{})~\cite{tanks},
which combines the share of the reconstruction lying within \SI{5}{cm} of the reference with the share of the
reference reconstructed within \SI{5}{cm}. Per frame, it is scored by AbsRel~\cite{eigen14}, the mean relative depth
error, and by the share of pixels whose predicted depth is within a factor of 1.25 of the true depth, which we call
\wone{}~\cite{eigen14}. For objective~(3), the share of the reference reconstructed within 2, 5 and \SI{10}{cm} stands for the
accuracy needed for renovation measurement, furniture placement and a room overview, respectively, and a use case
counts as served when that share reaches 90\,\%.

The scope is as follows. The data are home rooms of ARKitScenes captured with one iPad~Pro model (colour images of
$640\times480$ pixels, LiDAR depth of $256\times192$); the ground truth is the Faro depth distributed with the dataset,
and iPad LiDAR is never used as ground truth. Only the DPT head~\cite{dpt}, the depth decoder of DA-v2 Metric-Indoor
Large, is fine-tuned, with one training recipe, while its image encoder stays frozen. Six test rooms are evaluated as
meshes and per frame, and \rsVN{} further rooms per frame only, all disjoint from the rooms used for training and
checkpoint selection. Every depth source passes through the same offline pipeline---per-frame depth, scale alignment,
fusion into a truncated signed distance function (TSDF) volume~\cite{curless96}, meshing---so the depth source is the
only variable, and because every source and the reference share the camera poses recorded by ARKit, pose error is
excluded. Learned reconstruction systems~\cite{atlas,neuralrecon,simplerecon}, other phone cameras including Android,
real-time inference on the device and pose estimation are outside the scope.

Each objective has a matching expected benefit. The first tells an app maker whether data it can already collect from
Pro-device users improves room scans on devices without LiDAR, with no calibration or extra sensor at run time; the
second, whether labelling needs a laser scanner at all; the third, which product features a device without LiDAR can
offer, together with a pipeline in which a new depth source is evaluated by changing one configuration entry; and the
fourth, where to invest next---per-frame scale, training data or model size.
