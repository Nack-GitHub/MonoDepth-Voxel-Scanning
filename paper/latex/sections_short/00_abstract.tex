\begin{abstract}
Most phones have no LiDAR and must scan rooms with monocular depth, yet off-the-shelf ``metric'' models such as
Depth Anything~V2 (DA-v2) Metric-Indoor~\cite{da2}, trained on synthetic images, misplace a real room by
\SI{\rsMETRICCM}{cm}. We ask whether fine-tuning such a model with the phone's own LiDAR depth, with no laser scanner
involved, makes it more accurate than its pretrained self, measured against the Faro Focus S70 laser
scanner~\cite{faro} that provides the ground truth of ARKitScenes~\cite{arkitscenes}. We fine-tune only the DPT
head~\cite{dpt} (the depth decoder; the image encoder stays frozen) on 24 rooms with iPad LiDAR labels, giving
\ftmain{}, and compare it with the pretrained model in one pipeline---per-frame depth fused into a truncated signed
distance function (TSDF) volume~\cite{curless96}, then meshed---where the depth source is the only variable, on rooms
never used for training: six with a laser-scanned reference mesh and \rsVN{} more evaluated per frame. \ftmain{}
lowers the Chamfer distance~\cite{chamfer} (mean nearest-point distance between the reconstructed and reference
meshes, both ways) from \SI{\rsMETRICCM}{cm} to \SI{\rsFTLIDARALLCM}{cm} ($\rsMAINGAIN\times$, better in every room) and
AbsRel~\cite{eigen14} (mean relative depth error) from \rsVMETRICABSREL{} to \rsVFTALLABSREL{} (better in
\rsVWINS/\rsVN{} rooms), with no calibration or sensor at run time; a laser teacher is not measurably better. Its
remaining error is a scale that swings from view to view, which fine-tuning does not remove, so it still trails iPad
LiDAR (\SI{\rsLIDARCM}{cm}) and falls short of overview accuracy ($\geq$\,90\,\% of the room within \SI{10}{cm}).
\end{abstract}
