\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}
\textbf{Main question.} Is DA-v2 Metric-Indoor fine-tuned with the iPad's own LiDAR (R3) better than the same model
pretrained, when measured against the \emph{Faro laser scanner, the raw ground truth ARKitScenes ships}? Exp.~6 answers
it on six test rooms in 3D and 2D, Exp.~7 on 20 further rooms in 2D. Exp.~1--5 price the other depth sources through the
same pipeline as context for where monocular error comes from and where R3 stands relative to a sensor.

\subsection{Dataset: ARKitScenes}
We use ARKitScenes~\cite{arkitscenes} because it is the only dataset that provides \textbf{all three} things the
question needs for the same room: RGB video from a real device (iPad~Pro), depth from that device's LiDAR
(\code{lowres\_depth}, $256\times192$), and depth from a Faro laser scanner registered to the camera poses
(\code{highres\_depth}, $1920\times1440$). The ``iPhone~Pro today'' row and the reference therefore come from one scan,
with no cross-sensor alignment of our own.

\textbf{Ground truth in this paper is the dataset's Faro data as distributed}, not corrected or interpolated: in 2D we
compare per-frame depth with \code{highres\_depth} directly (nearest-neighbour down-sampling to the $256\times192$ grid,
so every GT value used is a value Faro measured, never an average); in 3D we compare with a reference mesh fused from
that same \code{highres\_depth} (\S\ref{sec:reference}), because the dataset ships no laser mesh. The iPad LiDAR
(\code{lowres\_depth}) is \emph{never} used as ground truth---only as the teacher of R2/R3 and as the \code{lidar} row.

Two dataset constraints shape the experiments: (1) Faro depth exists only for the frames Apple kept ($\sim$4~fps), so
every source is evaluated \textbf{on this same frame set} and Exp.~3 (frame stride) measures coverage more than
compute (\S\ref{sec:stride}); (2) no laser mesh is provided, so we build one (\S\ref{sec:reference}).

\subsection{Reference mesh}\label{sec:reference}
We fuse \code{highres\_depth} from \textbf{every} frame with TSDF at \SI{1}{cm} voxels (\code{sdf\_trunc} \SI{3}{cm})
once per scene into \code{reference\_mesh.ply}, and never touch it again. Consequences for reading the tables:
the \code{gt} row runs at the experiment's voxel size (\SI{4}{cm}) and therefore has non-zero Chamfer---the
\textbf{discretisation loss of the pipeline itself}, a lower bound for every row; the reference covers only what Faro
saw, so every source integrates only pixels where Faro has a value (\code{eval.mask\_to\_gt}); and the reference uses
the same poses as every row, so VIO pose drift is \textbf{removed from the comparison by design}---all numbers are the
effect of the depth source alone (limitation: \S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Scale-alignment protocol}
Every non-identity aligner fits an affine map \textbf{in inverse-depth space}: $(s,t)$ such that
$1/gt \approx s\cdot(1/pred) + t$, by trimmed least squares---three rounds, dropping the worst 20\% of residuals each
round---on pixels where both GT and prediction are valid within \SIrange{0.1}{5}{m}, then inverted back to metres.
\begin{table*}[t]
\centering\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXX@{}}
\toprule
Aligner & Fitted on & Interpretation \\
\midrule
\code{oracle\_frame} & that frame's GT depth, every frame & model ceiling (upper bound) \\
\code{per\_scene} & GT depth of \textbf{10 frames spread over the scan}, stacked, fitted once; one $(S,T)$ for all frames & upper bound of a one-off calibration \\
\code{sparse\_points} & 200 LiDAR pixels/frame at confidence 2 (VIO proxy), per frame; 2 rounds of 10\% trimming; $<20$ points $\Rightarrow$ reuse previous $(s,t)$ & upper bound of a VIO-based system \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table*}
\code{per\_scene} also uses GT to fit, so it is the \textbf{upper bound of one-off calibration}: a real system that
calibrates from a ruler or a few VIO points will do no better.

\subsection{Fine-tuning protocol (Exp.~6)}\label{sec:ftsetup}
\paragraph{Scenes} Disjoint at the \code{visit\_id} level from the six test scenes, drawn with seed 0 from the
ARKitScenes screening list: 10 \emph{training} scenes that have both a Faro scan and LiDAR (so R1 and R2 train on the same
\emph{rooms}), 14 further training scenes with LiDAR only (no laser scan exists for them), and 3 scenes from
the official Validation fold, with Faro, used for checkpoint selection. Six test scenes, 3 validation, 24 training;
no room, and no other capture of the same room, appears in two of those sets. The split file
(\path{configs/training/splits.yaml}) and the per-scene frame counts are released with the code.
\paragraph{Labels} R1 uses Faro \code{highres\_depth} (\num{3594} frames over 10 scenes); R2 uses the iPad's
\code{lowres\_depth} with ARKit confidence $\geq 1$ on the same 10 scenes (\num{8656} frames); \textbf{R3, the paper's
main model}, uses the same LiDAR labels over all 24 scenes (\num{19896} frames). R1's and R2's frames are \emph{not} the
same set: R1 iterates the Faro timestamps ($\sim$4~fps) while R2/R3 iterate every third \code{vga\_wide} frame
($\sim$10~fps), because LiDAR accompanies every colour frame; at equal steps R2 therefore sees $\sim$2.4$\times$ more
distinct views than R1 (read \S\ref{sec:finetune} with this in mind). Depth is
resampled to the RGB grid with nearest-neighbour only; pixels beyond \SI{5}{m} or without a label are masked out.
\paragraph{Optimisation} The DINOv2 encoder is frozen (\num{300}M parameters in \code{eval} mode); only the DPT neck
and the metric head train, with SiLog loss ($\beta = 0.15$), AdamW (lr \num{5e-5}, weight decay 0.01, 200 warm-up steps
then cosine), bf16 autocast, batch 2 with gradient accumulation 4, on $518\times518$ crops of images resized so the
short side is 518. R1 and R2 run \num{6000} steps, R3 \num{12000}; validation every 500 steps keeps the checkpoint with
the lowest AbsRel on the 3 validation scenes. A \num{6000}-step run takes about an hour on an RTX~3070 (\SI{3.2}{GB} of VRAM), R3 about two.
\paragraph{At test time} the fine-tuned models are plain \code{DepthSource} rows like any other: RGB in, metres out,
\code{aligner: identity}, no access to depth, poses or intrinsics. Input preparation is identical for the pretrained and
fine-tuned rows (upright rotation, short side 518 and long side rounded to a multiple of 14, i.e.\ $518\times686$,
ImageNet normalisation) and matches the training resolution, so the difference between the two rows is the head's
weights alone.

\subsection{Metrics}\label{sec:metrics}
\paragraph{3D (headline)} Following \code{metrics\_3d.py} exactly for every row: 200{,}000 points sampled uniformly on
the produced mesh and on the reference (seed 0); accuracy $=$ mean pred$\rightarrow$ref distance (did we build
something that is not there?), completeness $=$ ref$\rightarrow$pred (did we miss something?), \textbf{Chamfer $=$
their mean}; precision/recall/F-score at $\tau = $ \SI{2}{}/\SI{5}{}/\SI{10}{cm}---\textbf{\fscore{} is the headline}
(\SI{5}{cm} $\approx$ the tolerance for furniture placement); normal consistency $=$ mean $|n_{pred}\cdot n_{ref}|$ over
nearest pairs.
\paragraph{2D} Per frame, depth \emph{after} alignment vs.\ Faro \code{highres\_depth} on the $256\times192$ grid:
RMSE, AbsRel, $\delta_1$ (fraction with $\max(p/g, g/p) < 1.25$). \emph{Protocol~2} (the standard NYU/KITTI
definition): pixels are selected by the ground truth alone (\SI{0.1}{m} $\leq$ GT $\leq$ \SI{5}{m}) and by whether the
source produced a value (a LiDAR pixel masked by confidence is not a prediction); the prediction is then
\emph{clipped} to \SIrange{0.1}{5}{m} rather than dropped. An earlier draft also dropped pixels whose \emph{prediction}
left the range, which hides the largest errors of a model that over-estimates distance; every 2D number here was
recomputed with protocol~2. Every Exp.~1--6 row moved by less than 0.001 except Depth Pro, whose AbsRel rose from
1.04 to \rsDEPTHPROABSREL{} because it often predicts beyond \SI{5}{m}---exactly the case the old definition hid. Pixel-weighted within a room, then averaged over rooms. In Exp.~1--5 the 2D metrics
separate depth error from fusion error; in Exp.~6--7 they are the most direct measure of how right a model's distances
are against the laser.
\paragraph{Time} \code{time\_total\_s} $=$ data loading $+$ inference $+$ alignment $+$ fusion $+$ extraction on one
machine; inference is reported separately in \S\ref{sec:business}.

\paragraph{Exp.~7} The six test rooms are the only ones with a reference mesh, but 2D evaluation needs only the
dataset's Faro depth. We therefore add 20 rooms from the ARKitScenes Validation fold
(\path{configs/eval/valfold_2d.yaml}, \path{scripts/select_valfold_2d.py}, seed 0): scans of 30--\SI{150}{s} with
$\geq$\SI{40}{MB} of \code{highres\_depth}, one video per \code{visit\_id}, excluding every visit that belongs to the
fine-tuning train, validation or test sets or that also appears in the Training fold. These rooms decide nothing
(checkpoints were already chosen on the three validation rooms). They are evaluated with \code{roomscan eval2d}, which
uses the same frames, grid, mask and \code{depth\_metrics} call as the pipeline without fusing (tested to reproduce the
full pipeline's numbers), on every second Faro frame ($\sim$2~fps).

Every run is one resolved \code{config.yaml} plus \code{metrics.json} under
\path{experiments/results/<exp>/<scene>_<run>/} (all committed). Tables in \S\ref{sec:results} are generated by
\code{roomscan report} without manual editing; numbers are mean $\pm$ std over the six scenes, with per-scene tables in
\code{per\_scene.md}. Everything reproduces with \code{make reference}, \code{make sweep-exp1} \ldots{} \code{sweep-exp6},
\code{make eval-exp7}, \code{make report} after downloading the data.
